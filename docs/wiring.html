HUB75 → ESP32 Wiring

Connecting a P3 64×32 LED panel to an ESP32 DevKit board for a breadboard test setup.

1 · Find the right connector

A HUB75 panel has two identical 2×8 connectors: an input and an output. Data passes through the panel from one to the other. Plugging into the output gives a black screen — without damaging anything.

2 · Connector pinout

Standard IDC numbering: pins 1 and 2 sit side by side, then 3 and 4 below them, and so on. Pin 1 is marked on the board by a square pad, a triangle or a silkscreened “1”.

3 · Wire-by-wire summary

On the board, the silkscreened number is the GPIO number: P25, D25, IO25 and G25 all mean GPIO25. The physical pin order, however, is not sequential — read the silkscreen, do not count positions.

4 · Rotary encoder and tilt switch

Four inputs to add. The chosen pins avoid the 13 HUB75 signals, the strapping pins that can prevent boot, and the programming serial port.

GPIO34 has no internal pull-up

ESP32 pins 34 to 39 are input-only and have no internal pull-up or pull-down resistors. Without the 10 kΩ to 3V3, the input floats and the tilt switch will return random values. This is exactly what your KiCad schematic provides for.

For a quick breadboard test without a resistor, put the tilt switch on GPIO4 as INPUT_PULLUP instead — but move it back to GPIO34 for the final board, so that test and production stay identical.

The tilt switch circuit

Contact stateGPIO34 voltagedigitalRead
Open — resting position3.3 VHIGH
Closed — tilted0 VLOW

The logic is therefore inverted: LOW means “tilted”. Without the resistor, the input would be connected to nothing when the contact opens, and digitalRead would return noise.

A ball tilt switch bounces far more than a button — the ball rolls and makes several brief contacts before settling. Allow 50 to 100 ms of software debounce, versus 5 to 20 ms for an ordinary push button.

Keeping 5 V for the encoder

If your board routes +5 V near the encoder and you would rather not change that net, simply leave the module's + pin unconnected. The module's pull-up resistors then have no current path, and the ESP32's internal pull-ups do the job. The contacts still short CLK and DT to ground: it works the same, with two fewer components.

Pins to avoid

Still free: GPIO0, GPIO2, GPIO4, GPIO5, GPIO35, GPIO36, GPIO39. Three of them are traps — GPIO0 is the BOOT button and would interfere with uploading, and GPIO2 and GPIO5 are strapping pins: a contact closed at power-up would prevent the board from booting.

Debouncing

A mechanical encoder bounces for several milliseconds. Without filtering, one detent will produce two or three increments. Two 100 nF capacitors, CLK–GND and DT–GND, handle most of it in hardware; the rest is handled in software with an interrupt-driven state machine.

5 · Power

This is what decides whether the setup works or not. A P3 64×32 draws up to 4 A at full white — the power comes from the external supply, never from the ESP32.

What breaks the setup

Powering the panel from the ESP32's 5V or VIN pin pushes several amps through the DevKit's regulator and the USB connector traces. At best the board reboots in a loop; at worst the regulator fails.

If you want a single source, do the opposite: the 5 V supply feeds the panel and the ESP32's VIN pin — and in that case, unplug USB before powering up.

6 · Before first power-up

  • Common ground — at least one HUB75 GND pin connected to an ESP32 GND. Without it, nothing works.
  • Short ribbon — under 20 cm. Without the 74HCT245s, the noise margin at 3.3 V is small.
  • Low brightness — the sketch starts at 90/255. Only raise it once the power supply has been validated.
  • Terminal polarity — V+ on red, GND on black. Reversing them destroys the panel instantly.
  • Power the ESP32 first — so the panel inputs are not left floating at start-up.

The test setup does without the 74HCT245s

Most panels accept 3.3 V directly over a short ribbon. The level shifters in the KiCad schematic are still required on the final board: they guarantee the noise margin and allow a longer ribbon.

7 · If the image is wrong

Each symptom points to a specific group of wires.

Screen completely black
Wrong connector (try the other one), or an FM6126A driver: uncomment mxconfig.driver in the sketch.
Only one half lights up
The R2/G2/B2 trio (pins 5-6-7) or R1/G1/B1 (1-2-3) is not connected.
Colours swapped
The “colour bars” test should show red, green, blue, yellow, cyan, magenta, white from left to right. If not, two wires of an RGB trio are crossed.
Rows duplicated or scrambled
One of the address wires A, B, C or D (pins 9 to 12) is misplaced or missing.
Image shifted by one pixel
Set mxconfig.clkphase to true.
Panel very dim or off
OE (connector pin 15, GPIO33) is not connected. It is the signal that enables the display. GPIO15 is CLK.
Flicker, stray pixels
Ribbon too long or ground missing. Shorten it, and check GND continuity.
The ESP32 reboots in a loop
Insufficient 5 V supply, or the panel is drawing its current through the board. Go back over section 5.
The board no longer boots at all
GPIO12 is a strapping pin: if the panel pulls it high at power-up, the ESP32 does not boot. Move A_PIN to GPIO4 for testing (not GPIO5: it is a strapping pin too, see above).
